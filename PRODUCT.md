# Farewell Product Plan

## Positioning
A consumer social product for designing and sharing a personal farewell aesthetic and experience.

This is not a will, legal directive, or funeral home workflow.
It is a preference-expression product with emotional, social, and identity value.

## Core Thesis
Death planning products fail young users because they ask for seriousness before curiosity.
This product should earn engagement through taste, self-expression, and social identity first.

## Product Loop
1. User sees a playful prompt: `I designed my farewell. What would yours look like?`
2. User customizes a visual farewell profile quickly.
3. Product generates a vivid summary and share card.
4. Friends tap through and design their own.
5. Returning users get lightweight prompts to refresh preferences as their taste changes.

## Initial Information Architecture
1. Landing / hook
2. Quick-start designer
3. Live preview
4. Farewell summary
5. Share state
6. Optional account / trusted contacts later

## Milestones
### Milestone 1
- Brand direction
- Landing page
- Interactive designer
- Live preview
- Summary output
- Share CTA shell

### Milestone 2
- Account creation
- Save profile
- Trusted people nomination
- Revisit reminders

### Milestone 3
- Verification and access workflow
- Admin tools
- Monetization experiments

## UX Principles
- Start in seconds
- Prefer choices over forms
- Keep tone warm and identity-led
- Make every step visually rewarding
- Use playful copy, not institutional copy

## Product Risks
- Too much funeral language kills curiosity
- Too many options create form fatigue
- Over-indexing on aesthetics may reduce trust

## Recommendation
I think the strongest wedge is not `plan your funeral` but `design your final vibe`.
That phrasing is culturally lighter, more social, and more likely to trigger sharing.
We can keep `farewell` as the trusted product language while using lighter copy in discovery surfaces.
